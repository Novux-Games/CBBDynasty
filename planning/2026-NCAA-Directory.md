# **NCAA Division I Men's Basketball Alignment Directory and Structural Analysis**

The opening week of the 2026–27 college basketball season establishes a major structural baseline following the multi-year cycle of national realignment ratified between 2024 and 2026\. The operational roster for NCAA Division I men's basketball encompasses 365 active varsity programs competing across 32 athletic conferences. This alignment reflects the reconstitution of the Pac-12 Conference as an autonomous basketball entity, the formal sunsetting and rebranding of the legacy Western Athletic Conference charter into the all-sports United Athletic Conference, and several inter-conference realignments driven by broadcast contracts, geographic consolidation, and institutional financial shifts.

## **Institutional Census and Eligibility Architecture**

Understanding the competitive census of Division I men's basketball requires distinguishing between full institutional members possessing active NCAA postseason certification and institutions progressing through mandatory provisional reclassification periods. The competitive landscape as of November 8, 2026, includes 361 fully certified active Division I members and four provisional members advancing along the multi-year pipeline from NCAA Division II.

## **Structural Realignments and Regional Governance Cascades**

The operational model for 2026–27 crystallized around twenty-nine institutional transitions, one strategic conference dissolution and reincorporation, one voluntary downward institutional departure to Division III, and one new upward elevation from Division II.

### **Pac-12 Revival and Western Conference Transformations**

Following the departure of ten members to the Big Ten, Big 12, and ACC in 2024, the Pac-12 Conference navigated a two-year regulatory grace period utilizing Oregon State University and Washington State University as its sole custodial members. Both institutions spent the 2024–25 and 2025–26 basketball campaigns competing under a temporary affiliate scheduling agreement within the West Coast Conference (WCC) while conference leadership planned a rebuild.  
On July 1, 2026, the Pac-12 resumed autonomous operations as a nine-institution basketball league by adding five members from the Mountain West Conference (Boise State, Colorado State, Fresno State, San Diego State, and Utah State), Texas State from the Sun Belt Conference, and premier private non-football basketball power Gonzaga from the West Coast Conference.  
This expansion catalyzed a chain of structural adjustments throughout the western footprint:

> * The Mountain West Conference absorbed the loss of five core members by converting the University of Hawaiʻi from football-only associate status into a full multi-sport member, adding the University of Texas at El Paso (UTEP) from Conference USA, securing the University of California, Davis (UC Davis) from the Big West, and integrating Grand Canyon University following its departure from the Western Athletic Conference.  
> * The Big West Conference, having lost UC Davis and Hawaiʻi, replenished its numbers by integrating California Baptist University and Utah Valley University from the WAC, while also securing California State University, Sacramento (Sacramento State) from the Big Sky Conference.  
> * The Big Sky Conference resolved the departure of Sacramento State by adding Southern Utah University and Utah Tech University, reuniting those institutions with their traditional Intermountain West peers.  
> * The West Coast Conference, having concluded its temporary hosting partnership with Oregon State and Washington State and forfeited Gonzaga to the Pac-12, maintained a ten-team league by integrating the University of Denver from the Summit League alongside previously acquired Seattle University.

### **Formation of the United Athletic Conference**

The institutional dissolution of the Western Athletic Conference (WAC) name marks the end of a historic intercollegiate athletic brand founded in 1962\. Initially created in 2023 as an FCS single-sport football alliance between the ASUN and WAC, the United Athletic Conference (UAC) executed an all-sports merger on July 1, 2026\. The corporate infrastructure of the WAC assumed the UAC name, consolidating remaining non-FBS programs (Abilene Christian, Tarleton State, and UT Arlington) with former ASUN members (Austin Peay, Central Arkansas, Eastern Kentucky, North Alabama, and West Georgia) and Ohio Valley Conference member Little Rock into a unified nine-team multi-sport league.

### **Eastern and Mid-Major Adjustments**

Subtle realignments altered the geography of several mid-major and non-football leagues:

> * Northern Illinois University separated its athletic operations, transferring its non-football sports from the Mid-American Conference (MAC) to the Horizon League, reducing MAC basketball to twelve programs.  
> * Tennessee Tech University departed the Ohio Valley Conference to join the Southern Conference (SoCon), pushing the SoCon roster to eleven basketball institutions.  
> * Louisiana Tech University completed an accelerated buyout process with Conference USA to join the Sun Belt Conference on July 1, 2026, offsetting Texas State's exit to the Pac-12 and preserving the Sun Belt's fourteen-team roster.  
> * The Metro Atlantic Athletic Conference streamlined its institutional branding, formally dropping "Atlantic Athletic" to become the **Metro Conference**.  
> * Saint Francis University (PA) made the voluntary decision to exit the Northeast Conference and withdraw from NCAA Division I altogether, beginning its institutional transition downward to Division III within the Presidents' Athletic Conference (PAC).  
> * The University of West Florida became the newest institution to enter the Division I pipeline, launching its transition from Division II's Gulf South Conference to join the Atlantic Sun Conference.

## **Macro-Governance and Postseason Access Implications**

The formalization of the 32-conference architecture for 2026–27 carries critical ramifications for national governance, competitive scheduling, and NCAA tournament selection access. With the Pac-12 reconstituting its autonomous operation, the NCAA Division I Men's Basketball Championship restores its baseline of 32 automatic qualifying bids (AQ) reserved for conference postseason champions.  
During the two-season hiatus where the Pac-12 did not operate an independent basketball schedule, an additional at-large bid was introduced into the national pool, expanding the at-large cohort from 36 to 37 programs within the 68-team bracket. The re-entry of the Pac-12—particularly with national contenders such as Gonzaga, San Diego State, and Boise State serving as multi-bid anchors—contracts the at-large bubble, intensifying pressure on mid-tier multi-bid leagues including the Mountain West, Atlantic 10, and Missouri Valley.  
Concurrently, the competitive gulf between the Power Four conferences (ACC, Big Ten, Big 12, SEC) and the remaining Division I leagues has widened as a consequence of institutional resource redistribution. The Power Four leagues represent 68 institutions that capture the majority of Tier 1 media valuations and leverage maximum direct-to-athlete revenue allocations.  
In response, the reconstituted "Group of Six" (comprising the Pac-12, Mountain West, American, Sun Belt, Conference USA, and Mid-American) has recalibrated its scheduling formulas, reducing conference game requirements in select leagues to protect non-conference NET opportunities against Tier 1 opponents.  
The consolidation of regional mid-majors into travel-optimized configurations, as demonstrated by the establishment of the United Athletic Conference and the Big Sky's Intermountain focus, underscores a national trend where institutions outside the primary revenue core are designing structural alignments to lower travel overhead while preserving Division I membership equity.  
Barring further emergency realignments, the Division I baseline enters the 2026–27 basketball season stabilized at **32 conferences and 365 participating programs**.
